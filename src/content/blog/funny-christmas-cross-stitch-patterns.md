Some people hang a perfect felt angel on the tree. Others want a tired little pun in a hoop on the wall. If you are the second kind of stitcher (or you are stitching a gift for one), this list is for you.

Christmas is about seven weeks away, so there is time to stitch one or two of these and still wrap them. Every pattern below is an instant PDF download with a color chart, a symbol chart and a DMC thread list. If you want the classic and cozy side of the season too, see our [15 Christmas cross stitch patterns to start now](/blog/christmas-cross-stitch-patterns/).

## Who to give them to

Funny stitched gifts land best when the joke is about the person. A few matches that tend to work:

- **The tired parent or teacher:** "Jingle Some of the Way, I'm Tired" says what they cannot say out loud in December.
- **The friend who loves a pun:** Mistle Toad and Christmas Loading are pun-based and easy to hang on a wall or a tree.
- **The goth or horror fan:** Santa Ghost.
- **The chaotic one in the group chat:** the feral raccoon.
- **The cat person:** Christmas Cat, ideally with a real cat sitting next to it for the photo.

## Make a funny piece look polished

Humor looks even better when it is neatly finished. A few simple choices help:

- **Stitch on white or cream fabric** so the text is easy to read from across the room.
- **Pick a small hoop or a simple frame.** A plain black or natural wood frame makes a joke look like deliberate art.
- **Turn the tiny designs into ornaments.** Our guide to [finishing a cross stitch ornament](/blog/how-to-finish-a-cross-stitch-ornament/) shows three easy ways, including a mini hoop that takes about 15 minutes.
- **Check the size before you start.** Our [Aida count guide](/blog/aida-count-finished-size/) shows how to work out the finished size, so the piece fits the frame you already bought.

## Tips for stitching text and jokes

Many funny patterns rely on a few words, and a single misplaced stitch in lettering is easy to spot. These habits help:

- **Stitch the words first.** If the text is off-center, you can still adjust the rest of the design around it.
- **Count each letter separately.** Check a letter against the chart before moving to the next one, instead of counting a whole line at once.
- **Use a highlighter or sticky note on a printed chart.** Mark the row you are on so you never lose your place after a break.
- **Keep your cross direction consistent.** Every top stitch should go the same way, which makes letters look crisp.
- **Finish with backstitch last.** Thin outline stitches added at the end clean up the edges of letters and small features.

## How to stitch it in time

Pick a pattern with a small stitch count if you have only a few weeks left, and stitch a little each day rather than waiting for a free weekend. Start with the gift you most want to finish, and stitch for yourself after the presents are wrapped.

Happy stitching, and happy sarcasm.
