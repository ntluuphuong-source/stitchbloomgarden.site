A finished ornament is one of the best gifts a stitcher can give: it is small, quick and gets brought out every single December. The stitching is the easy part. The finishing, turning a square of fabric into something you can hang on a tree, is where most people get stuck.

Here are three ways to finish a cross stitch ornament, from easiest to most polished. None of them needs a sewing machine.

## Before you start: pick a small design

Ornaments look best when the design is about 2 to 4 inches across. On 14 count Aida that means roughly 30 to 55 stitches wide. Tiny motifs like the ones in our [Christmas Sampler with 20 ornament icons](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4587794157?utm_source=stitchbloomgarden.site&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=blog) or [Mini Christmas Motifs](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4579724096?utm_source=stitchbloomgarden.site&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=blog) are made for this.

When your stitching is done, wash it gently in cool water if it got grubby, roll it in a towel, then press it face down on a fluffy towel with a warm iron. Pressing face down keeps your stitches from flattening.

## Method 1: Felt-backed flat ornament (easiest)

This finish takes about 20 minutes and is great when you are making a lot of ornaments at once.

**You need:** felt, thin cardboard (a cereal box works), fabric glue or double-sided tape, ribbon, scissors.

1. Cut a piece of cardboard in the shape you want: a circle, square or heart, about half an inch bigger than your design on every side.
2. Lay your stitching face down, put the cardboard on top, and trim the fabric so about half an inch sticks out all around.
3. Fold the extra fabric over the back of the cardboard and glue or tape it down. On curves, snip little notches into the fabric so it folds flat.
4. Fold a 6 inch piece of ribbon into a loop and glue the ends to the top of the back.
5. Cut felt the same shape as the cardboard and glue it over the back to hide everything.

**Tip:** For a cleaner edge, glue a thin cord or ric-rac around the outside.

## Method 2: Mini hoop ornament (no cutting skills needed)

Small 3 or 4 inch embroidery hoops turn any stitching into an ornament in about 15 minutes. Wooden hoops look rustic, and you can paint them red, white or gold.

1. Center your stitching in the hoop and tighten the screw until the fabric is drum-tight.
2. Trim the extra fabric on the back, leaving about 1 inch all around.
3. Run a loose running stitch around the edge of that leftover fabric, pull it tight like a drawstring and knot it. The fabric gathers neatly behind the hoop.
4. Glue a circle of felt over the back.
5. Tie a ribbon through the screw at the top to hang it.

Our [Christmas Gnomes](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4584281363?utm_source=stitchbloomgarden.site&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=blog) look especially sweet in a 3 inch hoop.

## Method 3: Stuffed pillow ornament (most polished)

This is the classic finish you see in vintage ornaments. It takes about 45 minutes by hand.

**You need:** backing fabric (felt, cotton or another piece of Aida), a little polyester stuffing, needle and thread, ribbon.

1. Trim your stitching to a square or rectangle, leaving half an inch of plain fabric around the design.
2. Cut backing fabric the same size.
3. Place them face to face (pretty sides together) and sew around three sides with small backstitches, about a quarter inch from the edge.
4. Clip the corners, then turn it right side out through the open side. Push the corners out gently with a pencil.
5. Stuff lightly. Too much stuffing distorts the design.
6. Tuck in a ribbon loop at the top, fold the open edges inward and stitch the opening closed with small stitches.

**Shortcut:** Use felt for the backing and sew the two pieces together back to back with a blanket stitch around the outside. No turning needed, and the stitched edge becomes part of the look.

## Which method should you choose?

- **Making 10 ornaments for gifts or a swap:** felt-backed flat ornaments.
- **Want it done tonight:** a mini hoop.
- **A special keepsake for one person:** the stuffed pillow finish.
